Rejected or insufficient alternatives

One built-in universal Effect type

Problem: it turns unrelated control domains into members of one privileged hierarchy. It pressures the language toward broad ambient effect annotations and weakens the distinction between local private control and exported capabilities.

Top effect / any effect

Problem: convenient until it becomes a bin where precision goes to die. It makes unrelated handlers look interchangeable and gives poor guidance to lowering.

Erase the effect at every reset

Problem: unsound when the handler retains or forwards the continuation. The reset may have moved the obligation into a queue, closure, task object, or returned value.

All effects are ordinary monads

Problem: monadic encodings can represent many effects, but they do not directly solve delimited continuation capture, answer-type changes, local prompt matching, or optimization of single-shot continuations.

Global dynamic handler lookup

Problem: matching becomes ambient rather than identity-based. It is harder to see which handler catches which operation, and capability export is less explicit.

Only untyped shift/reset

Problem: useful locally, insufficient for async, exceptions, and library interfaces that need to state which control domains they require or export.

Only named effects

Problem: forces every local early exit and implementation detail into global vocabulary. Existentials prevent this signature pollution.

Require multi-shot continuations everywhere

Problem: makes the implementation pay for copying/reification even where a continuation is demonstrably linear and non-escaping.
